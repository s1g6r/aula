# Model benchmark

Run on 2026-09-26 against Featherless.ai (https://api.featherless.ai/v1) in **combined** mode (one call returns every language). Generated by `scripts/bench-models.ts`; raw outputs are in `docs/bench/raw-results.json`.

## What we measured

We sent 20 sentences written the way teachers actually talk (8 Biology, 6 Algebra, 6 US History) through the exact prompt and validation schema Aula uses live. Each call asks for Spanish, Arabic, Chinese (Simplified), Vietnamese at once, just like the real pipeline. Each call also includes the two previous sentences as context and the teacher's key terms. Calls ran one at a time, so the latency is what one lesson would see.

- **First token**: median time until the model starts answering.
- **First language ready**: median / 95th-percentile time until the first language is complete. Aula streams replies and sends each language to its students the moment it is finished, so this is what the largest language group waits for.
- **All languages ready**: median / 95th-percentile time until every language is complete. The first call to each model is reported separately as "cold", because Featherless may need to load the model.
- **Speed**: output tokens per second (median).
- **Valid JSON**: the reply parsed and passed our zod schema with every language present. Anything else would fall back to English for students.
- **Term recall**: of the teacher's key terms that appeared in a sentence, how many the model marked for highlighting.
- **Highlightable**: of the terms returned, how many can actually be found in the translated line (so we can highlight them).
- **ASR repairs**: two sentences contain deliberate speech-recognition mistakes ("sell membrane", "why intercept"). This counts how many the model fixed.
- **Units**: Featherless concurrency cost per request. Our plan has 4 units in total.

## What we learned

- **Parallel requests don't help on our plan.** Four glossary calls started at the same moment finished at about 11, 24, 37 and 48 seconds: Featherless worked through them one after another. A separate "split" run (one call per language, in parallel) was slower than one combined call. So Aula sends one call at a time per lesson and merges sentences that pile up.
- **Output length is the whole game.** Models wrote 17 to 30 tokens per second. Our first prompt asked for a definition with every term and took about 15 seconds for four languages. Moving definitions to a one-time glossary and asking for minified JSON cut the reply to about 200 tokens.
- **Cold starts are real.** The first call to each model took 7 to 14 seconds. Aula sends a tiny warm-up request when a lesson is created.
- **A "thinking" model is the wrong tool.** `Qwen3.5-9B` spent its output reasoning and never produced valid JSON (0 of 20).
- **Models over-mark terms.** Qwen listed every key term even for sentences that contained none, so Aula only keeps a term if its translation really appears in the line (the "Highlightable" column above is before that filter).
- **Repairs differ.** Gemma fixed both planted speech-recognition mistakes with a clean full sentence. Mistral fixed both but sometimes returned only a fragment ("cell membrane") or an arrow ("The why intercept → The y-intercept"), which would display badly. Qwen missed "why intercept".

## Quality notes

These judgments were made by Claude Code (the AI assistant building Aula) from the outputs and the back-translations above. They are a starting point, not a native-speaker review.

- **Spanish, Arabic, Chinese, Vietnamese:** all three working models keep the meaning. Differences are mostly wording ("doorman" vs "gatekeeper"). Mistral's Vietnamese was the weakest ("Bill of Rights" became "Freedom of the Rights", "Fifth Amendment" became "Article 5 of the Constitution").
- **Haitian Creole, Somali, Dari (beta):** Gemma is clearly best. Its Somali and Haitian Creole read as fluent, correct sentences. Qwen's Somali is not usable (it turned "ATP" into "ATM" and one line is broken repetition), and Mistral's Somali is also poor. All three produce reasonable Persian for Dari, though closer to Iranian Farsi vocabulary than Afghan Dari.
- **Glossaries:** Gemma and Mistral returned all 12 complete. Qwen returned 8 of 12 (two came back with a single term, one was invalid JSON, one timed out).

## Decision

**We route by language.** Chosen on Sep 26 after reviewing the results above.

- **Live captions: `Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507`** (1 unit). It was the fastest (first language 2.3s median) with 100% valid JSON and good Spanish, Arabic, Chinese and Vietnamese.
- **Somali, Haitian Creole and Dari: `google/gemma-4-26B-A4B-it`** (2 units). Qwen's Somali wasn't usable, and Gemma's was fluent. If any student in the room reads one of these "beta" languages, that lesson's translation calls go to Gemma (for every language in the call, so it stays one call).
- **Glossaries and recaps: Gemma.** They run once and aren't time-critical, and Gemma completed 12 of 12 glossaries against Qwen's 8.

**Updated Sep 27, after testers found Aula too slow** (details in `DECISIONS.md`, "Latency pass"). Beta languages now get their own Gemma call, so Spanish no longer waits for the slower model. Recaps turned out to be time-critical: students wait for them. Qwen now writes the English recap (about 10s against Gemma's 15s, with similar quality on the demo transcript). Definitions also moved to Qwen, one term per call (about 2.5s); in a retest, 28 of 30 calls were valid and 49 of 54 terms came back, and a second pass now asks again for any missing term. Gemma still handles Somali, Haitian Creole and Dari everywhere.

Rejected: Gemma everywhere (best quality, but the 4th language in a room waited about 10.6s) and Qwen everywhere (fastest, but it failed the students who most need help).

### Verified in a real lesson

After choosing, we ran real lessons through the finished app (`e2e/real-ai.spec.ts`): a teacher, two phones, four Biology sentences about 5 seconds apart, timed from the teacher's screen to the phone's screen.

| Room | Model used | 1st language (median) | 2nd language (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spanish + Arabic | Qwen | 2.3s | 3.3s |
| Spanish + Somali | Gemma | 3.3s | 6.8s |

The first sentence of a lesson is slower (up to about 6s) because the model is still warming up. These times include the test's own polling, so the real numbers are a little lower.

That run also caught a real bug: with two AI calls allowed in flight, a live caption could end up queued at Featherless behind a background glossary call, look "stalled", and be dropped. Aula now keeps one call in flight at a time and cancels background work (glossaries, warm-up) the moment a caption is waiting. See `DECISIONS.md`.
